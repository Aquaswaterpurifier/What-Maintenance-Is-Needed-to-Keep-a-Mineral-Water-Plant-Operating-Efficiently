## **1. Clean and Inspect Water Storage Tanks Regularly**

Water storage tanks play an important role in a mineral water plant. They store raw water before treatment and purified water after processing.

Over time, sediment, dirt, and microbial contamination can affect tank hygiene if proper cleaning procedures are not followed.

Important maintenance activities include:

- Inspecting tanks for dirt, sediment, and unwanted deposits.
- Cleaning and sanitising tanks according to a documented schedule.
- Checking tank covers and access points.
- Inspecting pipelines and connections for leakage.
- Ensuring that treated water storage tanks remain properly protected.
- Maintaining cleaning and sanitisation records.

Particular attention should be given to treated water storage because contamination at this stage can affect the quality of the final product.

For Coimbatore-based manufacturers, maintaining proper storage hygiene is essential, especially when supplying water to customers who expect consistent quality every day.

## **2. Maintain RO Membranes for Better Purification Performance**

Reverse Osmosis (RO) is commonly used in packaged drinking water plants to remove dissolved salts and other contaminants from water.

However, RO membranes require regular monitoring and maintenance. Over time, suspended particles, mineral deposits, organic matter, and biological growth can affect membrane performance.

Signs that your RO membrane may require attention include:

- Reduced purified water production.
- Increased operating pressure.
- Changes in permeate water quality.
- Higher salt passage.
- Increased energy consumption.

To maintain RO membranes effectively:

1. Monitor feed water and treated water quality.
2. Record operating pressure and flow rates.
3. Check the pressure difference across the membrane.
4. Monitor salt rejection performance.
5. Follow the manufacturer's cleaning recommendations.
6. Replace membranes when performance cannot be restored through appropriate cleaning.

Do not wait until the entire system stops working before inspecting the membranes.

A properly maintained RO system can help improve production consistency and reduce avoidable operating expenses.

## **3. Replace Pre-Filters at the Right Time**

Pre-filtration is an important stage in water treatment because it protects downstream equipment, especially RO membranes.

Most industrial water purification systems use different filtration stages depending on the raw water quality.

Common filtration components include:

**Pressure Sand Filter:** Helps remove suspended particles, sediment, and turbidity.

**Activated Carbon Filter:** Helps reduce chlorine, certain organic compounds, and unwanted taste and odour.

**Micron Filters:** Capture smaller particles before water enters sensitive treatment equipment.

These filters need periodic inspection, backwashing where applicable, and replacement according to operating conditions.

A few important maintenance practices include:

- Check inlet and outlet pressure readings.
- Monitor pressure differences across filters.
- Backwash sand and carbon filters according to the manufacturer's instructions.
- Inspect filter media for deterioration.
- Replace cartridge filters when required.
- Maintain a record of filter replacement dates.

If pre-filters are neglected, the RO membrane may experience unnecessary stress, increasing maintenance costs.

## **4. Inspect Pumps, Motors, and Electrical Components**

Pumps and motors are essential for maintaining water movement throughout the purification process.

A mineral water plant may use raw water pumps, high-pressure RO pumps, transfer pumps, and other supporting equipment.

If these components fail, the entire production process may be interrupted.

During routine inspections, check:

- Unusual noise or vibration.
- Water leakage around pump connections.
- Motor temperature.
- Electrical connections and control panels.
- Pressure and flow performance.
- Signs of corrosion.
- Abnormal power consumption.

Electrical panels should be inspected by qualified technicians. Protective devices, earthing, and electrical safety arrangements should also be checked.

For plants operating continuously in Coimbatore, preventive inspection can help reduce unexpected downtime and avoid costly emergency repairs.

## **5. Monitor Water Quality on a Regular Basis**

Maintaining water quality is one of the most important responsibilities of a packaged drinking water plant operator.

Even if the purification equipment is working normally, regular testing is necessary to confirm that the finished product meets the applicable safety and quality requirements.

Important water quality parameters include:

- **pH:** Helps monitor the chemical characteristics of water.
- **TDS:** Measures the total concentration of dissolved substances.
- **Turbidity:** Indicates the presence of suspended particles.
- **Microbiological quality:** Helps identify harmful microorganisms.
- **Chemical parameters:** Checks relevant contaminants based on source water and regulatory requirements.

Manufacturers should follow the FSSAI Scheme of Testing for Packaged Drinking Water and Mineral Water, effective from 1 January 2026, along with other applicable food safety requirements.

Do not depend entirely on a handheld TDS meter. TDS alone cannot establish whether water is safe for consumption.

Maintain proper testing records, calibration records, and corrective action reports. Use competent laboratories for the tests required under applicable regulations.

## **6. Clean and Maintain Bottle and Jar Washing Equipment**

In a packaged drinking water plant, bottle and jar washing equipment plays an important role in maintaining hygiene.

Reusable 20-litre water jars require particular attention because they may return from customers with dust, deposits, and other contaminants.

Poor cleaning practices can introduce contamination into the finished product.

Essential maintenance activities include:

- Inspecting jar washing machines before production.
- Cleaning washing tanks and spray nozzles.
- Checking water pressure and spray performance.
- Maintaining appropriate cleaning and sanitisation procedures.
- Inspecting conveyor belts and moving components.
- Checking filling nozzles for cleanliness.
- Replacing worn-out seals and damaged parts.

Also inspect reusable jars for cracks, scratches, damaged necks, and other defects. Containers that cannot be properly cleaned or sanitised should not be reused.

For businesses supplying apartments, offices, and commercial establishments across Coimbatore, consistent jar hygiene is essential for maintaining customer trust.

## **7. Maintain Filling and Packaging Machines**

Filling and packaging equipment must operate accurately and hygienically to prevent product contamination and production losses.

A packaged drinking water plant may use automatic or semi-automatic bottle filling, capping, sealing, labelling, and packaging machines.

Regular maintenance should include:

**Filling nozzles:** Clean and inspect nozzles to prevent contamination and uneven filling.

**Capping machines:** Check cap alignment, tightening performance, and mechanical condition.

**Conveyor systems:** Inspect belts, rollers, bearings, and motors.

**Labelling machines:** Check label alignment, sensors, and adhesive application.

**Sealing equipment:** Inspect sealing quality and replace damaged components.

**Control systems:** Verify that sensors and automation controls are functioning correctly.

Operators should also check for leakage, incorrect filling levels, loose caps, and damaged packaging before products leave the facility.

A properly maintained packaging line helps reduce rejected products and maintain a professional brand image.

## **8. Manage RO Reject Water and Wastewater Properly**

Reverse Osmosis systems generate reject water during the purification process. The amount depends on the feed water quality, membrane condition, system design, and operating recovery.

Ignoring reject water management can increase water wastage and create environmental compliance problems.

Consider these practices:

- Monitor the quantity of reject water generated.
- Inspect reject water pipelines for leakage.
- Identify permitted reuse opportunities after checking water suitability.
- Maintain proper drainage arrangements.
- Prevent untreated wastewater from entering inappropriate drainage systems.
- Follow applicable Tamil Nadu Pollution Control Board requirements.

Where technically and legally suitable, reject water may be considered for non-product applications such as certain cleaning activities or other permitted uses.

However, it should never be mixed with treated [drinking water](https://aquascbe.com/) or reused in the production process without an appropriate treatment design and regulatory approval.

## **9. Maintain the Disinfection System**

Disinfection is an important part of packaged drinking water treatment. Depending on the plant design, it may involve UV treatment, ozonation, or other approved processes.

These systems require regular inspection to ensure that the intended treatment performance is maintained.

For UV systems:

- Check UV lamp operating hours.
- Clean the quartz sleeve.
- Inspect the UV intensity monitoring system, where provided.
- Replace lamps according to manufacturer recommendations.
- Check the power supply and alarm functions.

For ozone systems:

- Inspect ozone generator performance.
- Check gas lines and connections.
- Monitor ozone dosing and contact arrangements.
- Verify the effectiveness of the treatment process.
- Follow equipment safety instructions.

Disinfection should be monitored and validated as part of the complete water treatment process. Simply installing a UV lamp or ozone generator does not automatically guarantee microbiological safety.


## **10. Train Employees to Handle Equipment Properly**

Even advanced purification equipment cannot perform efficiently if operators do not know how to use it correctly.

Employee training should be included in your plant maintenance plan.

Train workers to:

- Operate the purification system correctly.
- Understand normal pressure and flow readings.
- Identify unusual machine sounds and vibrations.
- Follow cleaning and sanitisation procedures.
- Handle chemicals safely.
- Maintain production and testing records.
- Identify contamination risks.
- Report equipment problems immediately.

Employees should also understand the importance of personal hygiene, clean uniforms, handwashing, and proper handling of packaging materials.

For growing packaged drinking water businesses in Coimbatore, trained employees can help improve productivity and reduce avoidable operational mistakes.

## **13. Reduce Electricity Consumption and Improve Plant Efficiency**

Electricity is one of the recurring operating expenses of a mineral water plant.

Pumps, motors, compressors, RO systems, and packaging machinery can consume considerable power during daily production.

Regular maintenance can help prevent unnecessary energy consumption.

Consider these practices:

- Use energy-efficient pumps and motors.
- Inspect pumps for pressure losses and leakage.
- Clean filters regularly to avoid unnecessary pressure drops.
- Monitor RO operating pressure.
- Switch off equipment when it is not required, following safe shutdown procedures.
- Maintain electrical components properly.
- Monitor daily electricity consumption.

You can also maintain an electricity consumption log and compare it with daily production. This helps identify whether the plant is using more energy than expected.

## **Common Mineral Water Plant Maintenance Mistakes to Avoid**

Some manufacturers focus on daily production and overlook maintenance until a problem occurs.

Avoid these common mistakes:

1. Ignoring changes in RO membrane performance.
2. Delaying filter replacement.
3. Neglecting water storage tank cleaning.
4. Using damaged or unhygienic water jars.
5. Ignoring unusual pump sounds and vibrations.
6. Failing to monitor water quality regularly.
7. Skipping preventive maintenance inspections.
8. Neglecting electrical safety checks.
9. Failing to maintain equipment service records.
10. Depending only on TDS readings to determine drinking water safety.

Avoiding these mistakes can help maintain a more dependable production process.
